Enhance this local Next.js app called Tax Final Confirmation at /Users/wengffung/dev/web/weng/app/taxes. It is an interactive final-check of a return that was already prepared. It is a cross-check, not tax advice, and it is not tied to FreeTaxUSA or any other tax platform. Do not name a tax platform in the notes. After each implementation, commit locally, report files changed and line counts, and offer a git diff. Do not push and do not open a pull request.

The chart, checklist, and preparation-cost comparison all come from one decision graph. Clicking a box answers it. Only the next nodes that apply are revealed. Unknown is always a choice, is drawn with a rust outline, and is not treated as yes. Check nodes name forms and lines to verify on the prepared return. They never tell the user to start a form. Thresholds and dollar figures come from a year-specific source. If the year or the source is missing, leave the figure blank and say so. Never assume a place or a dollar amount. "LA" stays unresolved until the user says Louisiana or Los Angeles.

Cover tax year, filing status (including married filing), wages, self-employment, payment apps, rentals, interest and dividends, capital gains, digital assets, retirement, HSA, education, estimated tax, dependents, a personal mortgage, age or blindness, being claimed as a dependent, standard versus itemized, and jurisdiction. Schedules flow onto Form 1040 and are labeled as lines to verify.

Self-employment: ask whether the work is a sole proprietorship, a disregarded LLC, a partnership, or an S corporation. A sole proprietorship can have more than one activity, and each activity is its own Schedule C. The self-employment note explains net profit, that 92.35% is generally subject to self-employment tax, the 15.3% rate (12.4% Social Security up to the wage base plus 2.9% Medicare), and that half the self-employment tax is deducted for income-tax purposes. The wage base must show its tax year and source, because it changes.

Payment apps: the only answers are yes, no, and unknown. Do not offer "probably too low to file." Goods-and-services payments are still reported when Venmo, PayPal, or a similar app does not issue a 1099-K, and they are entered manually. Check the 2025 filing threshold against an IRS source instead of hardcoding it. The recalled line is more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions; keep it only if the source agrees.

Rentals: one paid-off property, ownership share, debt, how long it has been rented, and whether the depreciation records are clean. The age of the depreciation schedule does not change the preparation fee. What matters is clean carry-forward records. A rental reported since 2019 should already show that schedule on prior returns. Missing or incorrect records can mean extra work and a higher fee, with no dollar amount added. Consistent reporting should not be much extra work.

Estimated tax: the question "Does the return include estimated tax payments?" has an info note. Estimated tax is tax already paid during the year when withholding did not cover it, including self-employment tax. On the return it is a credit, separate from withholding. Yes means the prepared return has payments to verify. It is not an instruction to file Form 1040-ES. Cite the IRS estimated-taxes page. Individuals generally must pay estimates if they expect to owe $1,000 or more at filing. That is about whether payments were required, not a finding that this return has them. Do not compute a penalty.

Capital gain: a short note that a capital gain is the profit from selling a capital asset, such as stocks, real estate, or crypto, for more than the purchase price.

Put an i on nodes and on the connecting lines. If a box has both choices and a note, the bottom panel has Answer and Information tabs, and the i opens Information instead of a toast. Otherwise the i opens a toast and Read more opens a modal with the source. Info buttons still work on a saved case.

Case studies open the chart on a saved path and preload the records they check. The first is "Coder/Nurse 1099 Example": tax year 2025, single, no mortgage, 1099-NECs for nursing and coding with no LLC, Venmo or PayPal goods and services with the amount unknown, one paid-off rental with the same tenants since 2019, and a $975 independent-firm quote. Louisiana versus Los Angeles, the Venmo amount, and whether the depreciation records are clean stay unknown. The second is "Joint W-2 & Crypto Example": married filing jointly, W-2 wages, no self-employment, no rental, and a crypto sale. It is an illustration, not a real person's return.

Preparation cost covers H&R Block, TurboTax, and an independent firm, from published prices. $975 is an example of what an independent firm might charge for self-employment plus rental income, which is Schedule C and Schedule E, not a simple 1040. It is not a tax-return amount. Do not treat it as a Los Angeles or Louisiana price. Missing depreciation records do not add a dollar figure to the quote.

Mermaid draws one path at a time. Self-employment and rentals can have more than one named record. The example has Nursing 1099 and Coding 1099. The bottom panel switches records. Each record keeps its own answers. The chart says which record it is checking.
