
If the IRS audits you and determines that an error on your tax return was an honest mistake, the penalty is generally minimal or none—as long as:
- You cooperate with the audit
- You correct the mistake (e.g., pay any additional tax owed)
- There is no evidence of fraud or intentional negligence

Here’s what might happen depending on the situation:
🔹 1. No Penalty
	If it was a simple math error or you misunderstood a rule (e.g., entered the wrong depreciation amount), the IRS may just correct it and bill you for the difference in tax owed plus interest.
🔹 2. Negligence Penalty (if the mistake shows carelessness)
	Penalty: 20% of the underpaid tax.
	Example: You didn't keep proper records or ignored clear rules.
	But this usually only applies if the IRS believes you should have known better (not for honest mistakes made in good faith).
🔹 3. Fraud Penalty (very unlikely for honest mistakes)
	Penalty: 75% of the underpaid tax.
	Only applied if they find intent to deceive.
🔹 4. Interest
	Regardless of penalties, you'll always owe interest on any unpaid tax.
	This is charged from the original due date of the return until the balance is paid.

✅ Good News: You Can Often Avoid Penalties
If the IRS believes your mistake was:
- In good faith
- Not due to negligence
- And you correct it promptly

They may waive penalties entirely.

You can also formally request a penalty abatement due to reasonable cause (like misunderstanding complex rules, relying on a tax preparer, or serious illness).
