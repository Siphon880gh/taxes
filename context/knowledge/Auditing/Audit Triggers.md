There are what are known as "IRS Audit Triggers" that could flag your tax return to be audited.

### Why Taxpayers Get Audited

Some audits are random, but most are triggered by the IRS’s DIF (Discriminant Information Function) system, which flags unusual returns. Knowing common red flags (Audit Triggers) helps you avoid or quickly resolve an audit.

---

### Common IRS Audit Triggers

#### 1. **Unreported Income**

Missing income—like from freelancing, cash jobs, crypto, or investments—raises red flags. The IRS matches your return to forms submitted by banks, employers, etc. Even income under $600 must be reported.

**Tips:**

- Save all W-2s, 1099s, and K-1s.
- Report exact amounts shown on forms.
- Make estimated payments if self-employed.

**Reminder:** Gifts aren’t taxable to the receiver, but the giver must report gifts over $17,000 (2023).

---

#### 2. **Improper Deductions, Losses, or Credits**

##### 🚗 **Vehicle Use**

Claim only business-related mileage or depreciation. Claiming 100% use of a personal car is a red flag. Keep a detailed mileage log.

##### 🍽️ **Meals**

Only deduct meals for legitimate business purposes. Keep a log of who, what, and why. (100% deductible in 2022, 50% in 2023.)

##### 🏠 **Home Office**

Only self-employed people—not employees—can claim it. The space must be used exclusively and regularly for business.

##### 📉 **Hobby Losses**

To deduct losses, the activity must be a business (profit 3 of 5 years, or 2 of 7 for horse-related activities). File Form 5213 if you're starting out.

##### ❤️ **Charitable Donations**

Donations much higher than average for your income draw scrutiny. Use registered 501(c)(3)s, get receipts, and file Form 8283 for noncash donations over $500.

##### 💸 **Earned Income Tax Credit**

Often misused. Double-check your income and dependent claims—each child can only be claimed once.

---

#### 3. **Suspicious Patterns**

- **Rounded numbers** (e.g., $100, $500) look suspicious.
- **Math errors or missing info** can trigger audits.
- **Unsigned returns** are invalid and returned.

---

#### 4. **IRS Audit Hot Spots**

- **Crypto & NFTs**: Must report using Form 8949 + Schedule D.
- **Cash businesses**: Especially risky if over $10K in cash is received—must file Form 8300.
- **COVID Retirement Withdrawals**: Must follow special reporting rules (CARES Act).
- **Foreign Accounts**: Must report via FinCEN 114 if total exceeds $10K or Form 8938 for assets over $50K.
- **Tax Avoidance Schemes**: Abusive setups (like micro-captive insurance) are high-risk. Seek legal help if involved.
- **Stripend contractors** - Will I Get Audited as a **Travel Nurse**? Yes, travel nurses do get audited by the IRS, and sometimes, you're at higher risk of an IRS audit as a travel nurse because of the high rate of nontaxable income vs. taxable income.

---

### Final Tips

Even honest returns can be audited. To prepare:

- Keep detailed records.
- Stay updated with IRS rules.
- Use a tax professional for complex returns.

If audited, you can often resolve it with proper documentation or by arranging a payment plan.
