
In the tax preparer world, worksheets and receipts go together when doing your taxes and keeping records. Receipts are proof of what you spent or earned. Worksheets help you total up those amounts and sort them into categories for your tax return. They make it easier to fill out forms like Schedule C or E. You should keep both in case the IRS asks to see how you got your numbers. They help back up your deductions and show your math.

---

### 🧾 **Receipts**

Receipts are **supporting documents**—proof that you actually spent money on something that might be deductible. Think of them as evidence you save in case the IRS asks for backup.

Examples:
- A receipt for office supplies
- A rent statement
- A mileage log for car use

---

### 📄 **Worksheets**

Worksheets are **calculation tools or summaries**. They help organize and tally up those receipts (and other relevant info) to prepare entries for tax forms. Worksheets are typically **not submitted to the IRS** but are often **kept in case of audit** or for your own recordkeeping.

Types of worksheets:
- **Business expense worksheet** – summarizing totals for categories like travel, meals, supplies
- **Rental property worksheet** – tracking rent income, repairs, depreciation, etc.
- **Safe Harbor for QBI or Real Estate** – documenting your qualification

---

### 🔁 How They Work Together

- You **track receipts and expenses throughout the year**.
    
- Then you **summarize them in worksheets**.
    
- Then those worksheet totals go into your tax return (Schedule C, Schedule E, etc.)
