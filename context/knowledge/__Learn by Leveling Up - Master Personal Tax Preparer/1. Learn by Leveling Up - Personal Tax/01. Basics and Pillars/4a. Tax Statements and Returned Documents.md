### 🧾 TAX STATEMENTS — What You Receive

These are documents **sent to you by organizations or platforms** you interacted with during the year. They report your income, insurance, or other financial data to **both you and the IRS**.

#### Common Tax Forms You Might Receive:

- **📄 1099-NEC** – From a company that hired you as an **independent contractor**. They report non-employee compensation (e.g., freelancing, gig work).
    
- **📄 W-2** – From a company that **employed you**. This means:
    - They controlled how and when you worked
    - They **withheld taxes** for income, Social Security, Medicare, etc.

- **📄 1099-K** – From payment platforms like **Venmo**, **CashApp**, **PayPal**, or **Shopify**, if you had high-volume **business-related transactions**.
    
- **📄 1095-A** – From **Covered California** (or other state health insurance marketplaces), showing:
    - Your health insurance coverage
    - Any **advance premium tax credits** you received

- **📄 1099-B, 1099-DIV, 1099-INT** – From **investment platforms** like Coinbase, Robinhood, or your stock broker, reporting:
    - Sales of stocks/crypto (1099-B)
    - Dividends (1099-DIV)
    - Interest income (1099-INT)
#### How You Might Receive Them:

- 📬 **Mailed to your home**
- 📧 **Emailed to you**
- 🔗 **Download from their website or app**
- 🏢 **Pick up in person (less common)**

### 📤 RETURNED DOCUMENTS — What the IRS Sees

These are copies of the same tax forms, but **sent directly to the IRS** by the employer, platform, or bank. They serve as verification for the information you report.

Examples:
- A **bank reports your dividends or interest** to the IRS
- An **employer submits your W-2**
- A **crypto platform reports your trading activity**

💡 If you forget to report these in your tax return, the IRS **already has the info** — and missing it increases your **chance of being audited**.
