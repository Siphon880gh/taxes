
**Learning objective:**
- Understand the process and importance of checking for updated tax filing methods, including changes to forms or how forms are used together (e.g., when one form line refers to another).

Over the years, the IRS has gone back and forth in its efforts to simplify the tax filing process. In some years, they streamlined the forms to make filing easier—sometimes making them so simple that important details were left out or misinterpreted. In response, later years saw the system expand again, reintroducing complexity in an effort to improve accuracy and cover more tax situations.

This ongoing tug-of-war means that tax forms are constantly changing—some being dropped, others added—depending on whether the IRS is prioritizing ease of use or thoroughness in a given year.
- Form 1040-SR is one such result: an optional version of Form 1040 tailored for taxpayers aged 65 and older.
SR stands for “Senior.”
- The discontinued Form 1040EZ, was a very simplified form for taxpayers with basic returns. Its role has been replaced by the more flexible Form 1040 and Form 1040-SR, which aim to simplify filing without losing too much detail.
