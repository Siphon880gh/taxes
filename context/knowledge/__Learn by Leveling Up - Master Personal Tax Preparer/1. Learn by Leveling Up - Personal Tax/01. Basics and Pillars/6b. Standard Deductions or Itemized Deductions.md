If your itemized deductions on Schedule A are less than the standard deduction, it's usually better to just take the standard deduction.

**How to take the standard deduction:** 
Enter the value of the standard deduction at line 12:
![[Pasted image 20250415024134.png]]

**How to take the itemized deduction:**
Complete Schedule A like line 12 refers to, then copy that number over to line 12. Let's just say my itemized deductions are at exactly $14,600 (just happens to be the standard deductions):
![[Pasted image 20250415024134.png]]

**Important:** Even if you take the standard deduction, you still need to account for any business income and expenses reported on a Schedule C (for example). Other business income could be rental income at Schedule D, and they have their own set of expenses. You want to reduce your taxable business income.

For an updated list of standard deviations, visit Irs.gov, then the user flow is Credits & Deductions top tab -> Information for Individuals menu item. Scroll down to Standard deduction amounts.

For tax year 2024, it was:
- $14,600 for single or married filing separately 
- $29,200 for married couples filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse 
- $21,900 for head of household
