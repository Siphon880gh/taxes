
The three main pillars in tax filing are:
- Income
- Deductions
- Credits

Definitions:
- **Income**: This is everything you earned—wages, self-employment income, rental income, interest, etc. It’s the starting point of your tax return.
- **Deductions**: These reduce how much of your income is actually taxable. Examples include the standard deduction, mortgage interest, and business expenses.
- **Credits**: These reduce the actual tax you owe, dollar-for-dollar. Some are refundable (you can get money back even if you owe nothing), like the Earned Income Credit or Child Tax Credit.

Income could include:
- Employee income
- Profits

Deductions and credits can include:
- Qualified Business Income deduction
- Premium tax credit
- Deductions towards rental income because of depreciation and contributing to tenants that cuts into your income (eg. Insurance, Shared Utilities portion, etc)

---

However a fourth pillar "Healthcare Insurance" now sort of exists:

Starting in 2014, under President Obama’s Affordable Care Act, most people were required to have health insurance all year or pay a penalty on their federal taxes. But beginning in 2019, President Trump changed that—there’s no longer a federal penalty for not having health insurance.

However, you are still responsible in other ways:
- **You may still qualify for the Premium Tax Credit (PTC)** from federal if you bought coverage through the Health Insurance Marketplace and meet the income rules.
- **If you received advance payments** of the Premium Tax Credit (PTC) to lower your monthly premiums, you must file **IRS Form 8962** to reconcile the amount. This could either **increase your tax bill** if you received too much, or **give you a refund** if you received too little.
- **Some states still require health insurance.** States like **California, Massachusetts, New Jersey, Rhode Island**, and **Washington D.C.** kept their own individual mandates after Donald Trump reversed the federal mandate. So even though the federal rule ended, you might still face a **state penalty** if you don’t have coverage.

Your tax liability is the total amount of taxes you owe to the IRS or your state government each year. Your income tax liability is determined by your earnings, filing status, deductions, and credits. Certain deductions can lower the amount of income taxed, and credits can further reduce how much you owe.
