### Earned Income Credit (EIC) Overview

Also known as EITC or EIC - the **Earned Income Tax Credit** is a tax break designed to support low- to moderate-income workers who live and work in the U.S. It can reduce the taxes you owe or even give you a refund. It helps those who are surviving day to day to stay afloat, while keeping things fair between taxpayer and IRS and while preventing people from gaming the system to exploit this credit.

The credit can apply to W2 employees or 1099 freelancer/contractors because they are both working individuals.

---

### Who Can Qualify?

If you earned less than:
- **$66,819** (Married Filing Jointly), or
- **$59,899** (Single, Head of Household, or Qualifying Surviving Spouse)  
    in **tax year 2024**, you may be eligible for the EITC.

You don’t need to have children to qualify, but **having qualifying children can greatly increase the amount** you receive.

Note EITC is the same as EIC.

---

### Basic Requirements

To qualify, you must:
- Be **age 25 to 64** (if you don’t have children),
- Have **earned income** (like wages or self-employment),
- **Not be claimed as a dependent** on someone else’s return.

---

### Additional Criteria to prevent gaming ETC (Earned income tax credit)

- You must have **lived in the U.S. for more than half the year**.  
  The rules don’t say this directly, but the idea is that the U.S. wants to give this help to people who are really living and working here—not just visiting or planning to take the money back to another country. It also helps stop people from claiming the credit if they don’t actually spend much time in the U.S.
    
- Your **investment income** must be **less than $11,000** (as of 2023).  
  This rule is in place because the credit is meant for people who rely on work income—not those earning a lot from stocks, property, or other investments.
    

---

### Where to Find It on Your Tax Forms

The EIC appears on:
- **Form 1040, Page 2, Line 27** (as of tax year 2023).
    
If you have qualifying children, you’ll also need to fill out **Schedule EIC**.  
If you don’t have children, the IRS will calculate the credit using the information on your 1040.
