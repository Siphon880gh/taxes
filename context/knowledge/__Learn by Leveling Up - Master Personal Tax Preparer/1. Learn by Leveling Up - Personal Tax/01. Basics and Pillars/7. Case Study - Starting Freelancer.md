An example thinking would be

Let’s say you quit your corporate job to follow your dreams of starting your own digital business. So you became a freelancer with no W2. 

Your first few pages of the 1040 will read like this:
- Your total income, you compare it against Owe tax table to see preliminarily how much taxes you owe the federal government:
	- How to use table: [[4b. Quickly assess owed tax and earned income credit]]
- Because America wants to help struggling day to day workers, they offer an earned income credit. You look at the earned income table to see if you met one of the ranges of where your income level needs to be. If you make even $1 more, you won't qualify.
	- How to use table: [[4b. Quickly assess owed tax and earned income credit]]
- You’re a low earning freelancer (20k) because you’re just starting your business. Seeing that you live in California, you MUST pay healthcare monthly, which is expensive every month so you apply through state government assisted health insurance marketplace: CoveredCalifornia . 
	- They give you a rebate every month to cover the premium costs as long as you qualified based on your income level. 
	- At the end of the year, Covered California generated a tax statement 1095 A. 
	- If you were actually not given enough rebates and you foot the bill higher than usual during the year for health insurance, on Form 1040, you earned \*Premium Tax Credit
- As a freelancer, you have no employer withholding tax for you. Even while working as a registry nurse as a 1099-NEC, the company treated you as a contractor and doesn't manage the taxes for you (no withholding whatsoever). Because you were new to this or you're willing to take the underpayment penalty, you did not pay IRS ahead of April 15th every quarter like employers have been doing with the withholding for their W2 employees (even when balance is $0). 
	- So therefore you must be the Employer portion of federal income, social security, and medicare, as well as the Employee's portion.
	- This comes to be about 7.65% + 7.65% (both portions) or 15% of your income. This is also known as the **Self-Employment Tax** and needs to be filed as a Schedule SE. Refer to [[Self Employee Tax]] for examples.
	- Your underpayment penalty is usually $20-40 for 20k range.

You can find all this information on the first few pages of Form 1040. Many of the lines include fine print directing you to other schedules that were used to calculate the amounts—typically showing how income was reduced by deductions and credits.

\* Premium - Recall that premiums are the payments you make to keep an insurance policy, whether it's health or auto. So it's a Premium Tax Credit that you get when the government under-assisted (with the monthly rebates) you at CoveredCalifornia or any state or federally assisted health insurance marketplace.
