Employee vs. Contractor: Withholding Is Everything  
- W-2 employee: Your employer automatically withholds federal income tax, Social Security, and Medicare throughout the year. This prevents underpayment penalty (discussed next).  

Contractor/self-employed (1099): No one withholds for you. You’re expected to:  
Pay federal income tax  
- Pay both halves of Social Security + Medicare = 15.3% self-employment tax  
- Both halves is "employee" half and employee half. You're both.

If you don't pay ahead every quarter, there's an underpayment penalty because you haven't acted like an employer that withholds income and pays the IRS quarterly for your employees, even if you are both the employer and the employee and you have no employees under you.  Every quarter, there won't be a balance but you can pay ahead at the IRS website. Here's how the underpayment penalty may look:
- Though 20k come it usually penalty of $20-40 on average.
- If it’s 40k income, the penalty is usually $100-200 on average.

If you made $20,000 as a contractor, here’s a rough breakdown:
- ~$1,530 (7.65%) is the "employee" portion
- ~$1,530 (7.65%) is the "employer" portion  
    → Total: **$3,060** self-employment tax  
    → Plus: **federal income tax**, maybe a few hundred depending on deductions, outside of the self-employment tax or Schedule SE.
- **Suggestion: Set aside ~25–30% of freelance income** for taxes. Set aside more (**even 45%**) if you're a high earner.

There's a maximum amount of self-employee income that gets taxed and that's prefilled on Schedule SE line 7 (Usually 160-180k in the early 2020s), so make sure to get the correct tax year's Schedule SE form. Calculations to arrive at the final self-employee tax will be partly based on line 7. Income that exceeds the line 7 means you are only tax on the line 7, so it is an upper level cap on how much you owe for self employment. If you’ve been paying quarterly estimates based on income **over** the cap:
- ✅ The **Social Security portion (12.4%) stops** at the cap.
- The **Medicare portion (2.9%) continues** on all income. There is no cap on Medicare.
- For a total of 15.3% that totals both employer and employee portion

Self Employment tax is not related to the federal income tax. The federal income tax is based on **total taxable income**, and it scales up with higher income levels using marginal tax brackets. There’s **no upper limit** — the more you earn, the more you may owe.
