
Schedule C aka Profit or Loss from Business (Mnemonic: Profit or Cost from Business)

Can include freelance work where you manage the payouts. Can include 1099-NEC where a company you consult or perform contractor work manages the payouts (and given you the 1099-NEC tax statement)

If it's for work you did on the side, such as consulting or any type of income you received for services you did and received no W2 tax statements, then it is reported on _Schedule C_ (even 1099-NEC and rental incomes). Self-employment taxes need to be paid (at Schedule SE) which are basically the federal income / medicare / social that a W2 employer withholds for you, and technically should be paid every quarter at IRS.gov even if balance is 0 to prevent a low underpayment penalty. To offset some of those taxes, see if your income (separately for rental and business) qualifies for the Qualified Business Income deduction (QBI) which is a separate form 8995 (Aka "Qualified Business Income Deduction - Simplified Computation"). You will likely not include rental income in Schedule C if you only rent out to one family because it's not day-to-day operation enough and that'll be cheaper, and although that makes rental wont qualify for QBI, it also means cheaper tax liability.

So for example, Schedule C could contain registry nursing, coding, and code tutoring. Excludes rental income if it doesn't require day-to-day operation (low volume tenants - just rented out to one family)

---

Schedule C does NOT include Employee W2 work because that's not equivalent to having your own business, and therefore won't have the same type of deductions, subtracting costs, and credits (especially Qualified Business Income credits). It includes 1099-NEC income and any income or profit that you couldn't get a 1099-NEC for.

Schedule C MAY OR MAY NOT include Rental Income. Passive: If either because you're a small landlord where the number of tenants doesn't necessitate day-to-day operation or because you're an investor who paid management to deal with day-to-day apartment renting, then it's passive income. Passive income stays in Schedule E Supplemental Income and Loss (Mnemonic: EZ passive income. Namesake Supplemental because often passive incomes are less than actively participating business incomes). Passive rental income also means you dont apply Safe Harbor for Rental QBI to apply for QBI, and it also means the rental income cannot apply for the QBI (So no rental income entry at form 8995 QBI). It's less tax liability this way.
