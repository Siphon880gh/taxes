 **De Minimis Safe Harbor** (For Business Owners0)
- You're saying:  
  👉 “I’m a small business owner. I bought items like tools, supplies, or office gear — each costing less than $2,500.  I don’t want to deal with capitalizing and depreciating them over years.   I’m electing the De Minimis Safe Harbor, attaching a statement to my return, and following the rules.”
- The IRS is saying:
  👉 “Okay, since your items are under $2,500 and you followed the safe harbor procedure, we won’t argue with you about treating those items as expenses instead of assets. You’re good — no need to capitalize or depreciate over each year of filing taxes.”

You can increase that threshold to $5000 if:

| If You Have...                | Threshold per Item |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------ |
| \*Audited financials + policy | $5,000             |
| No audited financials         | $2,500             |

\* Audited financials: [[Glossary - Audited Financials]]
\* Policy. There must be a written accounting policy in place before the start of that tax year you're filing stating that: "Items costing less than a specified amount (e.g., $5,000 or $2,500) will be expensed rather than capitalized for both book and tax purposes."
- Why? IRS wants to make sure regardless who the tax preparer is (you may switch tax preparer at different years), there won't be inconsistencies because items that have been purchased at specific tax years, have already been expensed, and will not be capitalized over future tax years, which unfairly gives the company more deductions.

Template:
```
(Treas. Reg. § 1.263(a)-1(f))

Name(s) as shown on Schedule E or C: {TAXPAYER NAME}
Social Security Number: {Social Security Number}
Address: {ADDRESS}
Tax Year: {YEAR}

By signing this tax return, I (we) elect to apply the De Minimis Safe Harbor under Treasury Regulation § 1.263(a)-1(f) for the tax year {YEAR}. I (we) affirm that the following conditions are met:

No applicable financial statements (AFS) were prepared for this taxpayer for the tax year.

The taxpayer has a written or consistent accounting policy to expense amounts paid for property costing $2,500 or less per item or invoice.

All qualifying expenses for tangible property during the tax year were treated as deductible expenses, not capitalized.

This election is made to treat such amounts as expenses for purposes of federal income taxation in accordance with the de minimis safe harbor provisions.

This election applies to all qualifying purchases made during the tax year, as permitted by IRS guidelines.

Signed: ___________________________
Date: _____________________________
```
