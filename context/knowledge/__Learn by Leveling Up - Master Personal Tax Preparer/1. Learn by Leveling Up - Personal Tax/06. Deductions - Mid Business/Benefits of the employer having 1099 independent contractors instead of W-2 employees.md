### ✅ **Benefits to the company (why they prefer 1099-NEC):**

1. **No payroll taxes:**
	- They **avoid paying their half** of:
	- **Social Security** (6.2%)
	- **Medicare** (1.45%)
	- Normally, an employer pays **7.65%** on top of your wages for these.

3. **No unemployment insurance (FUTA/SUTA):**
	- Employers don’t have to pay into **federal or state unemployment insurance** for 1099 workers.

5. **No workers’ compensation insurance**:
	- Most states require this for W-2 employees, but **not for independent contractors**.
	  
7. **No benefits**:
	- No obligation to provide **health insurance**, **sick leave**, **vacation**, or **retirement plans**.

9. **Lower admin costs**:
	- No payroll processing, W-4s, or employee tax filings.
	- Just issue a **1099-NEC** form if they pay you $600+ in the year.

---

### 😕 Downsides for the 1099 contractor:

- **They pay** the full **15.3% self-employment tax** (both employee and employer portions of Social Security + Medicare).
- **No included benefits**
- **No unemployment safety net** if they lose the contract.
- They're responsible for:
	- **Tracking income/expenses**
	- **Quarterly estimated taxes**
	  
- If you're very nice, you could prepare an education packet for your 1099 contractors

---

### 💡 Some companies misclassify:

Some employers may **misclassify workers as 1099** when the nature of the work is really employee-like (e.g., fixed schedules, control over how work is done). This is **illegal**, and the IRS and Department of Labor can **penalize companies** if caught.
