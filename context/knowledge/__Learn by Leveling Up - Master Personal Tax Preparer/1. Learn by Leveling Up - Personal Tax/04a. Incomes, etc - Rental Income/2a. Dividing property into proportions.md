If your county sends you **one annual property tax bill** that covers **both your primary residence and a rental property on the same lot**, it likely means you pay tax for both properties at the same time. It also could likely mean that your water bill and home insurance pays for the tenants'. Assuming you don't charge the tenants their portions, you need to properly **allocate the appropriate portion** when considering deductions of insurance, tax, etc.

There are two types of proportions:
- Headcounts is usually for shared water bill. eg. Two people live in the primary residence and Four tenants are renting, so the water bill you paid for their portion is 6/4 x year's water bill. That you can deduct because IRS wants to be fair.
- Area proportion is important. You divide the rented property on a map to figure out the percent that is rented. If you have square foot information on both properties and the plot, you may figure out the percent that way as well.

To estimate this percentage, you’ll need to divide the property into sections based on use. Here are a few tools that can help:
- **Google Maps satellite view** (for visual layout)
- Your county or city’s **assessor website**, which usually shows square footage and parcel maps  
  → Example: [L.A. County Assessor Property Search](https://assessor.lacounty.gov/homeowners/property-search)
- If you have sq foot information of the rented property versus the other property, that can work as well.

When is area proportion important? 
- Calculating the property tax you can deduct on the Rental Income (Schedule E). 
- Calculating the depreciating value of the property (rented area x (multiplied by) assessed building value when rent started and / (divided into) 27.5 years for residential rent - More information at [[4. Deducting depreciation for rental income]])
