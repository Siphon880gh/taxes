Schedule E aka Supplemental Income and Loss (Mnemonic: EZ passive income. Namesake Supplemental because often passive incomes are less than actively participating business incomes)

That income is more passive. You have to consider if your rental property necessitates day-to-day operation:
- No if low volume tenants (eg. renting out to one family) (If their crazy troublesome tenants causing issues daily, that still wont count)
- No if you are more of an investor owner that hired a management company to deal with the day-to-day operations directly.

Therefore Qualified business income does NOT apply to passive rental income.

If your rental income is active because you're hands on day-to-day, then you roll that rental income into Schedule C. You skip the Schedule E because that's for passive income (The form is even titled Supplemental Income to hint it's passive income because passive income is usually lower and supplementary)

And if your rental income is active, you can write it into the QBI 8995 Form. To decrease the chance of being audited, you can create a statement declaring Safe Harbor that you have done 250 hours of work and are involved day to day with the rental property business. But if you do get audited, you have to prove your 250 hours from your own logs. More information on that at [[5. Deducting Real Estate QBI for Rental Active Income]]
