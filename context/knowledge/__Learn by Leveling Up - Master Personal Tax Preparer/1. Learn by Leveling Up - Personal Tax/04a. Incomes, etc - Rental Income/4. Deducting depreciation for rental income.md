
For depreciation, you need the building/improvement assessed value in the year you started renting out the property. Let’s say you started renting out your property or part of the property on 2019 and let’s pretend this 2024 bill below is for 2019. 

IRS allows you to project the depreciation amount for the next 27.5 years from the year you rented out to residential (39 years for commercial renting out). It’s capped based on the Tax Reform Act of 1986 aiming to simplify depreciation rules. 

The depreciation is calculated from the Improvements portion of your 2019 bill and notice this is not the bill, it’s the assessment value! Alternately, you could reach out to the assessor office for the 2019’s building/improvement value. You can search your AIN or property at the assessor office for your county, for a chance they may list the year of rental service assessment (likely they will only list the present year). 

Pretending this bill is 2019, then: $68,300 is the assessed property of the building in 2019. We only rent 27% of the property, so we multiply 68,300 by .27 and that equals $18,441. Over 27.5 year, each year your rental property portion loses -$671 in value (that’s from 18,411 / 27.5). Then every year starting 2019 as long as there are tenants, you can claim depreciation credit at Schedule E (Rental Income) line 17

Q: What if you’re filing taxes for 2024 but you didn’t enter the depreciation values in 2019 the first year of starting rent? You still follow the projected depreciation for 2024. Any way to reclaim 2019's?
A: Firstly, this is called **"allowed or allowable"** depreciation - you’re treated as though you took the deduction, so you do not change the 2024’s depreciated value. You can still reclaim past depreciation you didn’t actually take by using Form 3115 (Change in Accounting Method) to “catch up” on missed depreciation.


Q: In the IRS form, there’s 27.5 years but there’s also convention code (eg. MM) and method code (eg. SL) in Form 4562 Depreciation and Amortization, associated with Schedule E (Rental Income). It’s under a section MACRS Depreciation. What are those?
A: That’s the straight method mid month approach we took with dividing 27.5 years starting from a basis year when the rental service started for the property. 
A: MACRS stands for Modified Accelerated Cost Recovery System. This means that the business can take larger tax deductions in the initial years and deduct less in later years of the asset’s life. That is the original intention although MACRS expands to include rental property which is more linear than it is greater deductions in the beginning. This form 4562 is not limited to juts rental property depreciation, and neither is MACRS limited to just rental properties. MACRS cover the conventions of MM, SL, Declining Balance, etc.

Q: After 28 years of filing the depreciation, can I continue to deduct depreciation?
A: Even if your property is still in great shape after 27.5 years, you **cannot depreciate it further** for tax purposes.
