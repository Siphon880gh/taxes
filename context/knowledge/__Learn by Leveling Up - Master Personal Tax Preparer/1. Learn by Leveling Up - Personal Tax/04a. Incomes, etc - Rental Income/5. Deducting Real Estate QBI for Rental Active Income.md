## 🛑 STOP!

If you rent out a property that qualifies as an active business where you're participating in its day to day operation, you can deduct 20%. If you're renting to low volume tenants (like renting to one family), it wont necessitate day-to-day operation, so that's considered passive income, and you CANNOT apply for QBI for this rental income because it's passive rental income that stays in Schedule E.

---


If you're a big business with many tenant families and you're not a hands-off investor paying management, then it's an active business. You would be able to apply for QBI deduction to help offset the struggles of running a business. This also means your Schedule C will include the rental income.

There are two paths to claiming the QBI deduction for your rental property - Safe Harbor or Not Safe Harbor
- If you **can hit 250 hours**, use Safe Harbor. It decreases your chance of being audited. Safe Harbor is saying "don't challenge me". But if you do get audited randomly, then you have to show proof of those 250 hours. Glossary: [[Glossary - Safe Harbor]].
- If you **can’t hit 250 hours**, **don’t attach the Safe Harbor statement**. Just claim the QBI deduction, and be prepared to prove **you're materially involved** if audited.

But to even qualify for Real Estate QBI deduction regardless:

💡**Examples that often qualify**:
- You’re a hands-on landlord: advertising, repairs, tenant screening.
- Multiple units or properties managed like a business.
- You keep books and records, track income and expenses.

🚫 **What may _not_ qualify**:
- Triple-net lease with no active management.
- Property rented to a relative below fair market value.
- Passive, one-off or hobby-like rental.

## With or Without Safe Harbor
Regardless, you need:

1. Report Rental Income on Schedule E (Form 1040)**
	- Line 3: Total rents received.
	- Line 18: Depreciation deduction (if applicable).
	- Line 21: Net income or loss.
  
2. Choose Form 8995 or 8995-A (QBI Deduction Form)
	- **Form 8995** – for simpler cases (taxable income < $191,950 single / $383,900 MFJ for 2024).
	- **Form 8995-A** – for more complex scenarios (higher income, multiple businesses, SSTB limits, etc.).

3. Complete Form 8995 or 8995-A (QBI Deduction Form)
	- Enter your **rental business name** (can be your name or “Rental of [address]”).
	- Report the **QBI income** (usually net profit from Schedule E).

## Without Safe Harbor

Simply do not create a statement to sign.

## Safe Harbor

You create a statement that you sign saying yes Im involved in the day to day repair and management of the rental property. And you're implying you are not not simply a rich investor that bought the rental property and then hired a management team to do everything.

It is considered safe harbor because the IRS backs off by you having this statement. Otherwise, you would have to file a lot more details. But it doesn't

Here's a template you can use, making sure we have the type of Statement, the property it's for, the tax payer signing, and the criteria for Real Estate QBI deduction met:

```
# Rental Real Estate QBI Safe Harbor Statement

Name(s) as shown on Schedule E: {TAX PAYER NAME}
Social Security Number: {Social Security Number}

Address: {ADDRESS}
Category: {Residential/Commercial}

By signing this tax return, I (we) affirm the rental real estate property described on this statement satisfies all requirements of the safe harbor set forth in Revenue Procedure 2019-38 for tax year {YEAR}. Specifically, I (we) affirm that all of the following are true:

- The property was not used as my (our) residence for any part of {YEAR}.

- Separate books and records were maintained to reflect rental income and expenses.

- 250 or more hours of rental services were performed for this property in one of the following time periods:

	- In {YEAR} (for rental properties in use for less than 4 years).  
	- Annually in any 3 of the prior 5 tax years (for rental properties in use for 4 years or more).

- Contemperaneous records were kept for this property regarding the following: 
	- Hours of all services performed  
	- Description of services performed  
	- Dates on which services were performed
	- Who performed the services  
	  
- The property was not under a triple net lease.  
- The home office deduction was not taken by the signee for the property.
```
