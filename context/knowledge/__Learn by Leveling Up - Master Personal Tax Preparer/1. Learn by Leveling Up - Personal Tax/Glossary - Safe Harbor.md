### Safe Harbor = "Safe from the IRS" (Mostly)

The term **"safe harbor"** means you're choosing to follow a **clear set of rules** laid out by the IRS — and **if you follow them**, the IRS **won’t challenge** you on that issue. You usually write a statement that's clearly titled for the particular Safe Harbor rule and then you sign it. However, it still does not prevent you from being audited because of other reasons or by a random selection, in which case the IRS may look into it regardless if there's a Safe Harbor signed.

In the case of **Rental Property QBI Safe harbor** (For Landlords):
- You're saying:  
    👉 “I followed **Rev. Proc. 2019-38**, did at least **250 hours of work**, kept records, and I'm signing a statement to prove it.”
- The IRS is saying:  
    👉 “Okay, if you’ve done that, we’ll **accept that your rental qualifies** for the QBI deduction — no need for us to dig deeper.”

In the case of **De Minimis Safe Harbor** (For Business Owners0)
- You're saying:  
  👉 “I’m a small business owner. I bought items like tools, supplies, or office gear — each costing less than $2,500.  I don’t want to deal with capitalizing and depreciating them over years.   I’m electing the De Minimis Safe Harbor, attaching a statement to my return, and following the rules.”
- The IRS is saying:
  👉 “Okay, since your items are under $2,500 and you followed the safe harbor procedure, we won’t argue with you about treating those items as expenses instead of assets. You’re good — no need to capitalize or depreciate over each year of filing taxes.”
