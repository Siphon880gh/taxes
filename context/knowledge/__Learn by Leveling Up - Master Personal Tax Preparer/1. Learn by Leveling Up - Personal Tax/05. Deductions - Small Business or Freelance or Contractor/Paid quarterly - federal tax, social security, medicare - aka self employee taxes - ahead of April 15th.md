 If you're 1099-NEC or reporting custom income that don't have 1099-NEC, there's no withholding, and then you will act as both Employer and Employee, paying double the Federal Taxes, and the Social Security, and the Medicare! This totals to about 15.3% and is dubbed the Self-Employee Tax (Schedule SE), and you will likely be penalized with an "Underpayment Penalty". To pay quarterly, you pay at IRS.gov ahead of the $0 balance (ID.me authorization requires you to show your face on web cam so a human can check your face against your ID card on their file).

But then how do I when I file my return indicate that I've paid those quarterly self employee taxes, so I do not have to risk paying double that?

**Form 1040 – Line 26:**
> This line is labeled **“Estimated tax payments and amount applied from prior year return.”**

This is where you report **ALL your estimated quarterly tax payments** you made during the year (including any overpayment from last year you applied forward).

 **💡 What to Gather Before You Enter**
- Your **IRS payment confirmations** (via IRS.gov account or bank records).
- Amounts and **dates** you paid: April, June, September, January (deadlines for Q1–Q4).

 🧾 **How This Affects Your Return**
- Those quarterly payments get **subtracted from your total tax owed**, reducing your **balance due**.
- If your payments **equal or exceed** what you owe for the year, **no penalty**.
- If you **underpaid**, you might see an **Underpayment Penalty** on **Form 2210** (but the software usually handles this automatically).
