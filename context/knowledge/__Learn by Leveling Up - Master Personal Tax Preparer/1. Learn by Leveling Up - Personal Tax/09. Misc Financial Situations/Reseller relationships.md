When you receive a tax statement 1099-NEC and you see Box 2 checkbox ticked: "Payer made direct sales totaling $5,000 or more of consumer products to recipient for resale"
- It’s there for **informational reporting**, required by the IRS.
- This box is checked by the company issuing the 1099-NEC to their contractors if they **sold $5,000 or more** **of consumer products directly** to the recipient, **not as an employee**, and those products were **intended for resale** (e.g. multi-level marketing, direct sales companies).

### 🔍 Example situations:
- The 1099-NEC issuer runs a direct sales business (e.g., Avon, Mary Kay, Herbalife).
- The 1099-NEC issuer gives someone $5,000+ worth of products in a year to sell on their behalf — like a distributor or affiliate.
- Even if the the 1099-NEC issuer **didn't pay them commissions** (which would go in Box 1), they **still check Box 2** to flag to the IRS that this resale relationship exists.

---

### 🧾 But wait — shouldn’t that go on Form 1099-MISC?

Yes, **traditionally this checkbox was on Form 1099-MISC**.  
But starting in 2020, **nonemployee compensation** got moved to **Form 1099-NEC**, and this checkbox followed to help match reporting needs.

Even though it’s not about _payments_, it's about **flagging resale relationships**.

---

### 🔁 For Employers working with contractors:

| Box       | What It's For            | When to Check                                       |
| --------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Box 1** | Nonemployee compensation | If you paid $600+ for services                      |
| **Box 2** | Direct sales indicator   | If you gave $5,000+ in consumer products for resale |

---


### ✅ Why Box 2 Exists (and Why the IRS Cares)

The **Box 2 checkbox** on Form 1099-NEC helps the IRS **flag and monitor direct sales arrangements**, especially those involving **consumer product resale**. This is mainly to prevent underreporting and scams:

1. **Tax avoidance by sellers**:  
    People who receive large amounts of resale inventory (e.g. $5,000+) might not report income from product sales or commissions unless the IRS is tipped off.
    
2. **Multi-level marketing (MLM) oversight**: 
   This rule largely targets **MLMs and direct sellers** where:
    - People recruit others.
    - Products are "purchased for resale."
    - Compensation may be commissions, bonuses, or even non-monetary incentives.
      
3. **Inventory dumping / fake business write-offs**:  
    Some may try to **claim big deductions** for "inventory costs" but never actually sell anything — or even resell the products to family members and write off the loss.
