​The **Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT)** is designed to ensure that high-income earners pay a minimum level of federal income tax, even if they have deductions or credits that significantly reduce their regular tax liability. It operates alongside the standard tax system, requiring taxpayers to calculate their taxes under both methods and pay the higher amount.​

IRS doesn't say it directly, but the intention is to prevent wealthy individuals from taking advantage of tax loopholes, even with skilled tax preparers.

### AMT Overview for 2025

**Exemption Amounts:**
- **Single or Head of Household:** $88,100
- **Married Filing Jointly:** $137,000
- **Married Filing Separately:** $68,500​

These exemptions begin to phase out at higher income levels:
- **Single or Head of Household:** $626,350
- **Married Filing Jointly:** $1,252,700
- **Married Filing Separately:** $626,350​

For every dollar your Alternative Minimum Taxable Income (AMTI) exceeds these thresholds, your exemption is reduced by 25 cents.​

**AMT Tax Rates:**
- **26%** on AMTI up to:
    - **Single or Head of Household:** $239,100
    - **Married Filing Jointly:** $239,100
    - **Married Filing Separately:** $119,550

- **28%** on AMTI exceeding these amounts​

To determine if you owe AMT, you'll need to complete IRS Form 6251, which recalculates your taxable income by adding back certain deductions and credits not allowed under AMT rules. If your AMT liability is higher than your regular tax liability, you must pay the higher amount.​
