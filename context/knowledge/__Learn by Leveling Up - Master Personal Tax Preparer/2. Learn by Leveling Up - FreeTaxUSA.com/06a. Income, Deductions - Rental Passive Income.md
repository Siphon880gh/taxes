## Deduct Rent Control Etc Fees (Eg. RSO, SCEP)

Rent control, rent certificate, and rental inspection fees (that you pay yearly for them to visit your tenants every 4 years to check for compliance with smoke detectors, etc) - All that can be deducted.

In Los Angeles, these yearly fees landlords have to pay are called RSO (Rent stabilization ordinance) and SCEP (Systematic Code Enforcement Inspection Program), in addition to annually registering their rental property in the Rental Registry (which is free).

This all goes into **Miscellaneous Expenses** at the bottom:

### **NOT** Management Fees
It does NOT go under Management fees. Management fees on Schedule E are specifically for amounts paid to a management company or individual for managing your rental property. This would include property management companies that handle tenant relations, maintenance coordination, etc.

Your rental property depreciates in value as people live in them. Depreciation is done at “Depreciable Assets”:

![[Pasted image 20250415043237.png]]

Answer questions to determine depreciating value for the tax year. You can get the assessed values at your property tax bill (paying county) or your county's assessor website (search google). At the county's assessor website, you search AIN or property address to get the value information. Technically you should use the build value (aka improvement value) assessed at the year you started renting the property (because the property starts depreciating when it's rented out). If you pay tax for a primary residence and rental property in one tax bill (or one AIN), then you need to take a fraction of it (proportionalize to the rental property's sq foot to the total property.). LA County: [https://assessor.lacounty.gov/homeowners/property-search](https://assessor.lacounty.gov/homeowners/property-search)

Learn the foundations of how depreciation is calculated at [[4. Deducting depreciation for rental income]]. You could have a spreadsheet somewhere that mapped what tax year what the depreciation value should be (At the 28th year, it should be halved the usual depreciation amount)

### Rental Income Depreciation Quirk

FreeTaxUSA calculates the building/improvement depreciation by asking for your original assessment values. No, it should be the assessment values on the date the property started being rented out because that's when it starts depreciating relative to the tenant use.
