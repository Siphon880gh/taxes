You have custom incomes/profits that don't generate tax statements like 1099-NEC. You can add these incomes to Schedule C after reviewing the 1099-NEC links

All freelancer, contractor, business income and profits goes Schedule C (Profits or Loss from Business)

Some such work can generate a tax statement to you by your company you're working (but not as a W2 employee), eg. 1099-NEC

Some work because it's unstructured or because of its nature, there is no tax statements generated. Unless you're working under the table with cash handed to you, IRS still has ways to detect irregularities if you don't report them.

All these incomes are technically you having your own business, even if you have not officially register a DBA or LLC.

Your Schedule C could have a mix of 1099-NEC and other custom incomes and profits that don't have tax statements associated with them. Again, you define the type of work and business name:
- [[Income as a Freelancer - Type of work or business name]]
- [[Schedule C - Line B - Code for Business or Professional Activity]]
